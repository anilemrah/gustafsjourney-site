Made in Växjö, the town that remembers the emigrants

Gustaf's Journey is made in Växjö, in the heart of Småland in southern Sweden. More emigrants left Småland for America than any other Swedish province, and this town still keeps their story.

A small team in Småland

We're a small, independent team, and Gustaf's Journey is our love letter to the Swedes who crossed the ocean and to the families who carried their stories forward. We wanted a word game that feels warm and unhurried, with a real story to come back to.

We also wanted every fact in it to be true. Every "Did you know?" card in the game is checked against sources such as museums and archives before it goes in. The pictures are drawn in our own style, and every Swedish word comes with its English meaning.

Småland, where so many left

Småland is a land of forests, lakes and stony fields. The ice age left its soil full of stones, and for hundreds of years farmers dug them out of their fields by hand and piled them into long stone walls. Many of those walls still wind through the forests where fields used to be.

Life on a small croft was hard. When letters came from America telling of plains so wide and flat that you could watch a wagon coming for hours, many decided to go. Småland sent more emigrants to America than any other Swedish province, and from Växjö station, train after train carried them towards the ships in Gothenburg.

The House of Emigrants

Here in Växjö stands the House of Emigrants, Utvandrarnas hus, opened in 1968. It tells the story of the more than a million Swedes who left for America. Its permanent exhibition, The New Land – the Dream of America, follows Swedish emigration from 1830 to 1930, and another exhibition is about the writer Vilhelm Moberg, whose novels told the story of emigrants from Småland.

The house holds the archive of the Swedish Emigrant Institute, founded in 1965: letters, diaries and photographs from both sides of the ocean. Every year, Americans come here to find where their families came from. In the pond outside, Carl Milles's emigrants ride across the sea on the back of a great fish.

Planning a visit? Opening hours and exhibitions are on the Kulturparken Småland website.

Did you know?From 1869, every emigrant leaving from Gothenburg had to be written down by the port police: name, age, home parish and where they were going. Today many of the old emigrant records can be searched from anywhere in the world.

Växjö in the game

In chapter 7 it's the summer of 1890, and Gustaf follows his friend Johan home to a croft near Växjö, where the fields seem to grow more stones than potatoes. He helps with the harvest, picks blueberries in the pine forest and dances every dance with Johan's sister Hilda.

Along the way you'll visit the stone cathedral, begun in the 1100s; Smålands museum, Sweden's oldest provincial museum, which opened its new building that very summer; the Kosta glassworks, where glass has been blown since 1742; the ruins of Kronoberg Castle; and the station where the emigrant trains left.

The real places from chapter 7, as they look today.

Why the cathedral looks different in the game

If you know Växjö today, the cathedral in the game may surprise you. The game is set in 1890, and it shows the church as Gustaf saw it: light walls and a single tower with stepped gables and clock faces. Lightning had burned its two tall spires back in 1740.

In a great restoration from 1957 to 1960, the cathedral got its two slender green spires back. The walls were painted dark red, after the restorers found traces of that old colour on them.

Much later in the story, Gustaf's great-granddaughter Karin comes to Sweden from Minnesota. She hires a bicycle and rides along the lake to the House of Emigrants, to search the old records for her own family.
